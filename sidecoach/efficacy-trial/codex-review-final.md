The reporting is mostly disciplined, but a few phrases are too clean.

1. **Headline:** acceptable only if read as “no confirmatory/pre-registered detection.” It is not proof of no effect. The M2 bootstrap CI is real evidence of a lower *mean* axe count, but it is not the registered confirmatory test and should not override the Wilcoxon/Holm result. Calling a CI-based victory a measure-swap is right. Calling the CI “weaker” is too glib: it answers a different estimand, and mean violations may be substantively meaningful. Best wording: “M2 is suggestive of a small mean reduction in axe violations, but the pre-registered Wilcoxon test did not reject.”

2. **Screenshot judges:** the 16/17 and 17/17 placebo preference deserves serious weight as a strong warning signal, not as a definitive harm claim. The author ruled out side-position bias and first-viewport framing. The artefact not ruled out is **visual identity leakage / style-template detectability**: sidecoach may produce a recognizable “sidecoach aesthetic” such as oversized serif headings, spacious editorial layouts, balanced wraps, press animations, tabular numerals, or other checklist-driven polish. A same-family judge may be reacting against that recognizable style rather than judging human preference.

3. **M3:** “teaching to the test, measured” is the right primary characterization for the six literal checklist/string rules dropping to zero. There is also a legitimate improvement reading for `skipped-heading`, `gray-on-color`, and the axe-corroborated heading/landmark results. But for the six polish rules, the scanner checks exactly what the payload names, so treating that as independent product-quality evidence would be circular.

4. **Supported / not supported:** mostly sound, but needs caveats:
   - “No large improvement” is okay for M1, less clean globally because M2 shows suggestive axe improvement and M3 shows checklist improvement.
   - “Prompt-length confound … small as an effect” is a bit overstated. With n=17, P vs C failing to move does not prove the length effect is small; it rules out only large detectable effects here.
   - “Sidecoach effect is therefore not a length artefact” is too strong. Say “not explained by any detected length effect in this run.”
   - “Rendered appearance measurably worsens” should stay secondary/exploratory/same-family every time it is stated.

5. **Post-hoc cleanup:** yes. The full-page variant is correctly labelled exploratory, but the report uses it rhetorically to strengthen the anti-sidecoach result. That is still post-hoc evidence and should be framed as a robustness probe, not a replication. The wrapper-less placebo inclusion is less troubling if truly decided before outcomes, but it should say whether arm identity was known then; if the author knew it was placebo, that is still a subjective retention call affecting S vs P.

Bottom line: the confirmatory verdict is sound. The M2 CI should be reported as suggestive, not buried and not used to claim success. The screenshot result is the most actionable warning, but the missing artefact is same-family/style leakage: the judge may be punishing a recognizable sidecoach-shaped visual pattern, not measuring real user preference.
