Who should attend

You don't need a portfolio, a pitch deck, or three years of coding behind you. You need a weekend and a willingness to build with strangers who'll stop being strangers by Saturday afternoon.

  • First event, first team

    Never built anything outside a classroom? Most first-time attendees say this on Friday. Mentors and team leads are there to get you moving, not judge your starting point.

  • Computer science & engineering

    Bring the build skills. Teams need people who can turn an idea into working code, hardware, or a working prototype by Sunday.

  • Business & design

    Bring the framing. Teams need people who can shape the idea, design the interface, and make the pitch land.

  • Undeclared & exploring

    Haven't picked a major yet? This is a low-stakes way to find out what kind of work pulls at you.

Schedule overview

Three days, three phases: form a team, build, then show what you made.

  1. Friday 6:00 PM to 9:00 PM

    Kickoff & team formation

    Opening talk, challenge prompt reveal, and the team-matching mixer for anyone registering solo.

  2. Saturday 9:00 AM to 9:00 PM

    Build day

    Skill workshops in the morning, open build time all day, mentors circulating through the room, meals provided.

  3. Sunday 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM

    Showcase & awards

    Teams pitch to judges, projects go up for a public walkthrough, then awards and closing remarks.

Team formation

Registering without a team is the default, not the exception. Most students arrive without one.

  • Friday's mixer groups you by interest and skill so you're not picking randomly.
  • Teams run 3 to 5 people. Mixed-major teams tend to build the most complete projects, but any combination works.
  • Already have a team? Note it on the registration form and we'll seat you together.

Prizes & support

You're not on your own for 48 hours, and the prizes reward more than just the polished demo.

Prize categories

  • Best overall build
  • Most creative use of the prompt
  • Best first-time team
  • People's choice, voted by attendees

Support resources

  • Mentors from local industry and faculty, circulating all day Saturday

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Teams need people who can build, but also people who can design, write, plan, and pitch. If you can commit the time, there's a role for you.

What if I don't have a team yet?

That's the default. Register solo and come to Friday's mixer, which is built specifically for matching people up.

How much does it cost?

Registration is free for all currently enrolled students. Meals, workshops, and materials are included.

What should I bring?

A laptop if you have one (a few loaner laptops are available), a phone charger, and anything you'd want for a late night on campus. The building stays open past midnight, but nobody's required to stay.

Can I leave and come back?

Yes. Badge scan-in gets you back into the building any time during event hours.

Is this only for students planning to start a company?

No. Most teams are prototyping ideas for the challenge, not founding companies. Some do keep working on the project afterward, but that's optional.
