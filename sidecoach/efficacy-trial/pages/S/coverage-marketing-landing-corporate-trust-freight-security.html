The problem

Audit risk hides in paperwork, not in your operation.

Most carriers run a secure operation and still fail an audit. The gap is rarely the work itself. It is where the proof of that work lives, and whether anyone can produce it on request.

01
Chain-of-custody is split across three systems.

Pickup and delivery confirmations live in drivers' phones, dispatch email threads, and paper logs at the dock, so no single record tells the full story.

02
Carrier insurance is checked once, then trusted for years.

Coverage lapses between onboarding reviews go unnoticed until a claim or an audit surfaces them.

03
Incidents get written up after memory has faded.

A theft or damage event reported two weeks late reads as inconsistent, even when the underlying facts are accurate.

02

Security and data handling

Compliance data is sensitive. It is treated that way.

The same records that protect you in an audit deserve their own protection.

1

Encrypted in transit and at rest

Every record, document, and message is encrypted end to end, both moving between systems and while stored.

2

Role-based access control

Access is scoped by role and least-privilege by default, so users see only the loads and carriers relevant to their work.

3

Immutable audit trail

Every change to a compliance record is logged with who made it and when, and the log itself cannot be edited.

4

Configurable data residency

Storage location is set per contract to match your regulatory and customer requirements.

5

Defined retention and deletion

Retention periods are set explicitly per record type, with deletion schedules you control.

6

Access reviewed on a fixed cadence

Account access is reviewed on a schedule set in your contract, not left open indefinitely after a role change.
