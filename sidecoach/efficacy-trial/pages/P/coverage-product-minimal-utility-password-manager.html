What VaultNest does

The basics you'd expect from a password manager, done in a straightforward way.

Encrypted vault

Passwords, secure notes, and card details are encrypted on your device before they're ever sent anywhere.

Autofill

Fills login forms in your browser and on your phone, so you're not copying and pasting passwords by hand.

Password generator

Creates a unique password for each site and flags logins where you've reused or repeated one.

Sync across devices

The same vault on your laptop, phone, and browser extension, kept up to date automatically.

Secure sharing

Share access to a login with someone else without ever showing them the password itself.

Breach alerts

Checks your saved logins against known public breach lists and tells you which ones to change.

Built for one person or a small team

The same vault, set up differently depending on who's using it.

Personal use

  • One vault across your phone, laptop, and browser

How your data is protected

In plain terms: your vault is locked with a key only you have, and the system is built so we never see that key.

Encrypted before it leaves your device

Your vault is encrypted locally using AES-256, a widely used encryption standard, before it's ever sent to our servers.

Only you know the master password

Your master password unlocks your vault on your device. We don't store it, and we can't recover it if you lose it - that's part of how the protection works.

Two-factor authentication

You can require a second step - an authenticator app or a hardware key - before your account can sign in anywhere new.

Independent security review

VaultNest's system is reviewed periodically by outside security researchers, and a summary of what was checked is published for customers.

What this does and does not mean: encryption keeps your vault unreadable if our servers were ever accessed without authorization. No security system removes every risk, which is why a strong master password and two-factor authentication both matter.

Importing your passwords and getting set up

Most people are fully switched over in under fifteen minutes.

  1. Create your account and master password

    Choose a master password you don't use anywhere else. This is the one password you'll need to remember.

  2. Install the extension and app

    Add the browser extension and, if you want, the mobile app so your vault is available wherever you sign in.

  3. Import your existing passwords

    Bring in logins from your browser's built-in password manager or from another password manager using a CSV file.

  4. Turn on autofill and two-factor authentication

    Let VaultNest fill logins for you, and add a second sign-in step to your VaultNest account itself.

Import sources currently supported

  • Chrome and Chromium-based browsers
  • Firefox
  • Safari
  • 1Password (exported CSV)
  • LastPass (exported CSV)
  • Bitwarden (exported CSV)
  • Any password manager that exports a CSV file

What happens if I forget my master password?

We can't see or reset your master password, since we never receive it in the first place. You can set up an emergency contact ahead of time so someone you trust can help you regain access.

Can VaultNest see my passwords?

No. Your vault is encrypted on your device before it's sent to our servers, so what we store is encrypted data we can't read.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your vault stays intact on your other devices. From another signed-in device or the web, you can remove the lost phone's access to your account.

Can I switch plans or cancel later?

Yes. You can move between plans or cancel at any time from your account settings, and your data stays exportable if you decide to leave.

Is there a way to try Personal or Team before paying?

Both paid plans include a 14-day trial with full features, and you won't be charged if you cancel before the trial ends.
