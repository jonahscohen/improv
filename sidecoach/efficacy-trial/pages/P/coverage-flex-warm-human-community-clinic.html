Community Clinic Appointment Guide

Everything you need to know about getting care at Harbor Street Health, in plain language - what we offer, what it costs, and how to book a visit.

Not sure where to start? That's okay. You do not need insurance, a diagnosis, or an appointment already booked to read this page or to call us. This guide will help you figure out if we're the right fit and what to do next.

Services we offer

Harbor Street Health is a primary care clinic. Here's what we can help with.

Primary and preventive care

Checkups, physicals, vaccinations, and ongoing care for chronic conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure.

Urgent, non-emergency concerns

Colds, infections, minor injuries, and other issues that need attention soon but aren't life-threatening.

Women's and reproductive health

Well-woman exams, birth control counseling, and pregnancy testing.

Behavioral health support

Screening and referrals for anxiety, depression, and stress-related concerns.

Basic labs and screenings

Bloodwork, blood pressure checks, and common screenings ordered on site.

Help finding specialty care

If you need a specialist, our staff will help you find one and make the referral.

Eligibility and payment

We do not turn people away for lack of insurance or lack of funds.

  • Everyone is welcome. You do not need to be a citizen or show immigration status to be seen.
  • Insured? We accept Medicaid, Medicare, and most major private insurance plans. Bring your insurance card if you have one.
  • Uninsured or underinsured? We offer a sliding-scale fee based on household income - the less you make, the less you pay. Fees can be as low as a few dollars per visit.
  • Can't pay at all today? Tell the front desk. We will still see you and work out payment afterward.

No one is denied care here because of an inability to pay.

Walk-ins are seen in the order they arrive, alongside scheduled appointments, so there may be a short wait. Arriving earlier in the day usually means a shorter wait.

Walk-in visits are best for things that need attention soon but are not emergencies - a cold that won't go away, a minor injury, or a new symptom you're worried about.

If this feels like a medical emergency (chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, or anything life-threatening), please call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room instead of waiting for a walk-in slot.

Language support

Care should make sense to you, in the language you're most comfortable in.

  • English
  • Spanish
  • Haitian Creole
  • Vietnamese
  • Mandarin
  • American Sign Language

Free interpreter services are available for these languages and many others, in person and by phone, for every visit. Just let us know your preferred language when you call or check in - there is no extra cost and no need to bring your own interpreter.

What to bring

Bring what you have - missing something is not a reason to reschedule.

  • A photo ID, if you have one (not required)
  • Insurance card, if you have coverage
  • A list of current medications, or the bottles themselves
  • Any recent medical records you may have
  • A list of questions or concerns you want to cover
  • A friend or family member for support, if that helps

If you don't have any of these, come anyway. We'll work with what you have.
