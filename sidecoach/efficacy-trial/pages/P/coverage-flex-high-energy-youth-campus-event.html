Who should attend

If you're curious, you already qualify.

BuildBurst isn't just for computer science majors or people who've done this before. It's built for a room full of different backgrounds working on the same problem.

1

First-timers

Never been to an event like this? Most attendees haven't either. Friday's kickoff is built to get everyone oriented before anything is expected of you.

2

Any major, not just tech

Business, design, health sciences, communications, undecided. The strongest teams last year mixed disciplines. Bring your major's perspective, not a coding resume.

3

Builders and non-builders

Some roles need code. Others need research, storytelling, design, or pitching. Every team needs people who aren't touching a keyboard the whole time.

4

Solo or with friends

Come with a group, come with one friend, or come alone. Team formation on Friday night is designed specifically for people arriving without a team.

Not sure you're "technical enough"? Neither were most of last year's participants before Friday night. Mentors and workshops are built for exactly that starting point.

Team formation

Show up without a team. That's normal.

Roughly half of every BuildBurst cohort arrives without a team already formed. The Friday mixer exists specifically to solve that, and organizers stay in the room to help quieter attendees find a fit.

Teams can also form ahead of time through the group chat that opens after registration, if you'd rather sort things out in advance.

  • Team size: 1 to 5 people

    Register solo, as a pair, or as a full group. There's no penalty for smaller teams. Judging accounts for team size.

  • Friday night pitch board

    Have half an idea but no group? Pin it to the pitch board and let others opt in, or browse ideas looking for a builder, designer, or researcher.

  • No idea yet? Also fine.

    Every challenge track ships with three starter prompts. Teams are welcome to adopt one instead of inventing something from scratch.

  • Cross-major matching

    On request, organizers will help balance a team so it isn't five people from the same major and skill set.

Prizes & support

What you walk away with, win or not.

Every team leaves with a working prototype, a practiced pitch, and mentor feedback. Prizes are a bonus on top of that.

Grand prize $2,500 + mentorship package

Awarded to the strongest overall build, judged on execution, clarity of the pitch, and real-world relevance.

Best social impact $750

For the project with the clearest positive effect on a campus or community problem.

Best first-time team $500

Reserved for teams where every member is attending a build event for the first time.

People's choice $500

Voted on by all attendees during Sunday's demo session, independent of the judging panel.

Support resources included for everyone

  • All meals covered. Dinner Friday through lunch Sunday, plus snacks.
  • 24-hour mentor lounge. Industry volunteers and faculty on hand for technical, design, and business questions.
  • Beginner workshop track. Intro sessions on rapid prototyping, no-code tools, and pitching, running in parallel all Saturday.
  • Quiet rest space. A low-light room for breaks, with the event never requiring an all-nighter.
  • Event swag. T-shirt, notebook, and stickers for every registered attendee.
FAQ

Still deciding? Start here.

Do I need coding experience to join?

No. Teams need a mix of skills, not identical ones. Design, research, writing, and pitching are just as central as building. The Saturday workshop track is built for attendees with no technical background.

What if I don't have a team yet?

Come anyway. The Friday mixer and pitch board exist specifically for solo attendees, and organizers are on hand to help you find a fit if you don't want to navigate it alone.

Is there a cost to attend?

No. Registration is free and includes all meals, workshop access, and event swag for the full weekend.

Do I have to stay the whole weekend?

No. Attend Friday and Saturday only, or join Saturday morning if Friday doesn't work. Just let your team know your availability during check-in so schedules can be planned around it.

What should I bring?

A laptop or tablet if you have one, a phone charger, and a water bottle. Nothing else is required. Notebooks and basic supplies are provided at check-in.

How are teams judged?

A panel scores each team on the working prototype, the clarity of the Sunday pitch, and real-world relevance of the idea. Team size and experience level are factored in, so smaller and first-time teams compete on even footing.
