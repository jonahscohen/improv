What it does

The features you'll actually use

VaultNest keeps the core job simple: store logins, fill them in, and warn you when one needs attention.

01

Autofill and capture

When you sign up for something new, VaultNest offers to save it. When you return, it fills the username and password field for you.

02

Built-in password generator

Create a long, random password with one click when you sign up or update an old, weak one.

03

Weak and reused password checks

Your vault is scanned for passwords that are short, reused across sites, or have appeared in a known breach, with a plain list of what to fix first.

04

Secure sharing

Share a single login with a teammate or family member without ever showing them the actual password text.

05

Works across your devices

Browser extension for Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari, plus apps for desktop and mobile, all kept in sync.

06

Secure notes and documents

Store Wi-Fi passwords, software licenses, or scanned IDs alongside your logins, protected the same way.

How it's protected

Security, in plain language

We'd rather explain this clearly than impress you with jargon. Here's what actually happens to your data.

  • Your vault is encrypted before it leaves your device

    VaultNest scrambles your passwords on your phone or computer, using your master password as the key, before anything is sent to our servers.

  • We don't store your master password

    Your master password never travels to us and isn't kept anywhere on our servers. That also means if you forget it, we can't read it back to you.

  • Your data is unreadable to us

    Because encryption happens on your device, what we store looks like scrambled text, even to VaultNest staff. This design is often called "zero-knowledge."

  • Two-factor login is available on every plan

    Add a second step, like a code from an authenticator app, so a stolen master password alone isn't enough to get in.

What this means for you

No security setup removes all risk, and we won't tell you otherwise. What this approach does is limit what a break-in at VaultNest could expose, and it puts the master password entirely in your hands. Choosing a strong, memorable master password and turning on two-factor login are the two things that matter most on your end.

If you ever forget your master password, recovery depends on the backup method you set up in advance, since we have no way to reset it for you.

Getting started

Switching over doesn't take long

Most people are set up and filling their first password within about ten minutes.

  1. Create your account

    Choose a master password. This is the only password you'll need to remember from now on, so pick one that's long and personal to you.

  2. Install the browser extension or app

    Add VaultNest to your browser, or install the desktop and mobile apps, then sign in once.

  3. Import your existing passwords

    Bring in passwords already saved in your browser or another password manager in a single import step.

  4. Turn on two-factor login

    Add an authenticator app as a second step so your account isn't protected by the master password alone.

What happens if I forget my master password?

Because we never store it, we can't reset it for you directly. Recovery depends on the backup option you set up when creating your account, such as a recovery code kept somewhere safe. Set this up early, before you need it.

Can I try it before paying?

Yes. The Free plan has no time limit and covers unlimited passwords on one device. Personal and Team both include a 30-day trial of their extra features before any charge.

What happens to my data if I cancel?

You can export your vault to a file at any time, including after cancelling, so your passwords are never locked in.

Does switching mean re-entering everything by hand?

No. The import step reads an exported file from your browser or previous password manager, so your existing entries carry over instead of being retyped.

Who can see passwords I share with a teammate?

Only people you explicitly share an entry with can use it, and only within the app itself. Sharing doesn't display the password as plain text to the recipient.
